Physics Complete Summary

A comprehensive journey through physics: from classical mechanics to quantum fields, with essential formulas, laws, and real‑world connections.

1. Foundations & Measurements

Physics describes the fundamental laws of nature using quantitative models. It relies on precise measurements and standardized units.

SI Base Units

  • Length: meter (m)
  • Mass: kilogram (kg)
  • Time: second (s)
  • Electric current: ampere (A)
  • Temperature: kelvin (K)
  • Amount of substance: mole (mol)
  • Luminous intensity: candela (cd)

Key Prefixes

nano (10⁻⁹), micro (10⁻⁶), milli (10⁻³), centi (10⁻²), kilo (10³), mega (10⁶), giga (10⁹).

Scalars vs Vectors

Scalar: magnitude only (mass, speed).
Vector: magnitude + direction (velocity, force).

2. Classical Mechanics

Kinematics

v = u + at        # final velocity
s = ut + ½at²   # displacement with constant acceleration
v² = u² + 2as    # velocity‑displacement relation

Newton's Laws

  • 1st: An object stays at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by a net force.
  • 2nd: F = ma (force equals mass times acceleration).
  • 3rd: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.

Work, Energy & Power

  • Kinetic energy: K = ½mv²
  • Potential energy (gravity): U = mgh
  • Work: W = F·d·cosθ
  • Power: P = W/t

Momentum & Collisions

p = mv. In isolated systems, total momentum is conserved. Elastic collisions conserve kinetic energy; inelastic ones don't.

3. Rotational Motion & Gravity

Angular Quantities

  • Angular velocity: ω = Δθ/Δt
  • Torque: τ = r × F
  • Moment of inertia: I = Σmr²
  • Angular momentum: L = Iω

Universal Gravitation

Newton's law: F = G·(m₁m₂)/r², where G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg².

Kepler's laws describe planetary orbits. Gravitational potential energy: U = -G·(m₁m₂)/r.

4. Fluid Mechanics

Pressure

P = F/A (Pascal's principle). Hydrostatic pressure: P = ρgh.

Buoyancy

Archimedes' principle: buoyant force equals weight of displaced fluid.

Continuity & Bernoulli

A₁v₁ = A₂v₂ (incompressible). Bernoulli's equation: P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant.

5. Thermodynamics

Laws of Thermodynamics

  • 0th: Thermal equilibrium → temperature.
  • 1st: ΔU = Q - W (energy conservation).
  • 2nd: Entropy of isolated system never decreases; no perfect engine.
  • 3rd: Absolute zero is unattainable.

Key Concepts

  • Heat capacity: Q = mcΔT or nCΔT.
  • Ideal gas law: PV = nRT (R = 8.314 J/mol·K).
  • Kinetic theory: K_avg = (3/2)kT where k = 1.38×10⁻²³ J/K.
Thermodynamic processes: isothermal (ΔT=0), adiabatic (Q=0), isobaric, isochoric.

6. Waves & Oscillations

Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)

x(t) = A·cos(ωt + φ), where ω = 2πf. For a spring: ω = √(k/m).

Wave Properties

  • Speed: v = f·λ.
  • Types: transverse (light) / longitudinal (sound).
  • Interference, diffraction, standing waves.

Sound

Speed in air ≈ 343 m/s. Intensity level: β = 10 log₁₀(I/I₀) dB. Doppler effect changes perceived frequency.

7. Electromagnetism

Electrostatics

Coulomb's law: F = k·(q₁q₂)/r² (k ≈ 9×10⁹ N·m²/C²). Electric field: E = F/q.

Circuits

  • Ohm's law: V = IR.
  • Power: P = IV = I²R = V²/R.
  • Capacitance: C = Q/V. Time constant τ = RC.

Magnetism

Lorentz force: F = q(v × B). Ampere's law, Faraday's law of induction.

Maxwell's Equations

Gauss's law for electricity & magnetism, Faraday's law, Ampère‑Maxwell law. Predicted electromagnetic waves (light).

8. Optics

Reflection & Refraction

Snell's law: n₁ sinθ₁ = n₂ sinθ₂. Total internal reflection when θ₁ > critical angle.

Lenses & Mirrors

Thin lens equation: 1/f = 1/do + 1/di. Magnification: m = -di/do.

Wave Optics

Double‑slit interference: d sinθ = mλ (maxima). Diffraction grating, thin‑film interference.

9. Relativity

Special Relativity

Postulates: laws of physics same in all inertial frames; speed of light c is constant.

  • Time dilation: Δt = γΔt₀ where γ = 1/√(1 - v²/c²).
  • Length contraction: L = L₀/γ.
  • Mass‑energy equivalence: E = mc².

General Relativity

Gravity as curvature of spacetime. Predicts gravitational waves, black holes, and expansion of universe.

10. Quantum Mechanics

Key Principles

  • Wave‑particle duality (de Broglie wavelength λ = h/p).
  • Uncertainty principle: Δx·Δp ≥ ℏ/2.
  • Schrödinger equation: iℏ ∂Ψ/∂t = ĤΨ.

Quantum Numbers

n (principal), l (azimuthal), m_l (magnetic), m_s (spin). Pauli exclusion principle.

11. Nuclear & Particle Physics

Radioactivity

Alpha, beta, gamma decay. Half‑life: N = N₀·(½)^(t/T₁/₂).

Standard Model

Quarks, leptons, gauge bosons, Higgs boson. Fundamental forces: electromagnetic, weak, strong, gravity.

Fission & Fusion

Binding energy curve. Nuclear power (fission) and stellar energy (fusion).

12. Cosmology & Astrophysics

  • Hubble's law: v = H₀·d (expansion of universe).
  • Cosmic microwave background radiation.
  • Dark matter & dark energy.
  • Stellar evolution (HR diagram, neutron stars, black holes).

14. Quick Tips & Common Pitfalls

Units

Always convert to SI before calculations. Check unit consistency in formulas.

Vectors

Don't forget direction; use components correctly. Force is a vector.

Sign conventions

In kinematics, gravity often negative; in optics, sign conventions for mirrors/lenses matter.

Energy conservation

Only holds in isolated systems; non‑conservative forces (friction) dissipate energy.

Common mistakes: using weight (N) instead of mass (kg), forgetting air resistance, mixing up series/parallel rules, and ignoring relativistic effects at high speeds.
